# Pilot and batch decisions

The pilot preceded the primary batch. It used the shared materials, coefficient routines, RK subroutines and saturation validation, with zero initial fields/inversion. Constant weak-probe, periodic 1.10 gain, strong-probe and slow-edge controls were included. results.json reports bit-identical native intensities between original coefficient allocations and the allocation-free N-dependent row update. The explicit periodic target was checked against (1-cos(2*pi*t/50))/2 rather than relying on generic sin defaults.

Representative 100-ps weak constant controls took approximately 0.4–0.6 s and 200-ps periodic controls approximately 0.8–1.3 s in the concurrent pilot. These measurements supported a 16-worker, resumable campaign expected to take minutes, with slower frequency-edge cases dominating. All-step float64 intensity retention implied tens of GB; only display/state records use practical coarser cadence. Actual primary integration/compression wall time was ~150 s for 2223 runs; the 762 follow-ups took about another two minutes. Lossless repacking and round-trip verification took ~253 s. The final full numerical campaign occupies approximately 47 GiB; the review bundle intentionally excludes these large native records, which remain beside their parameter/provenance snapshots.

The initial edge pilot accepted an approximately field-free plateau before the historically very slow emission onset. That pilot outcome is not a valid emission turn-on result and is excluded from scan results. The production scan starts convergence testing only after 1.2*historical_onset+40 ps. For historical onsets too long to observe under the 2000-ps cap, results remain incomplete. This guard is a practical observation choice, not an independent physical theorem about onset time.

Native metrics are computed at every integration step, independent of exported state cadence. Both branches of the Fig. 5 pair have half-step controls; one also has unchanged-step every-step output. Six pulse gains have additional half-step runs. A later strong-probe half-step control quantifies sensitivity at E0/Esat=1. All results and exact commands are retained; production commands and validation are in the parent tables/ and runs/ directories.

Normalized timestep 1 corresponds to ~6.85637e-5 ps. The inherited split inversion/field update and post-update-state/pre-update-time-label convention are retained. These checks do not establish a coupled fourth-order convergence rate. The default pointwise strong-probe difference on halving dt is about 0.0321% of peak intensity; final pulse widths in the six periodic checks differ by less than 5e-9 ps, while saved native peak times differ by at most one original step. Such small differences are results for these deterministic trajectories, not generic accuracy guarantees.
